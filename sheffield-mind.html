Sheffield Mind

A community campaign connecting purpose, participation and long-term support.

Project overview

A marketing and outreach campaign designed to increase awareness of Sheffield Mind, encourage community participation and explore a more sustainable supporter model. The documented campaign budget was £500.

Portfolio contextThe brief identifies the following campaign visuals as reconstructed or proposed portfolio executions. They are presented as concepts, not as verified historical campaign deliverables.

01 / The challenge

Make support feel like participation.

The campaign needed to increase awareness of Sheffield Mind and give people a stronger reason to engage over time. Volunteering, training, events and networking transformed the proposition from a traditional donation message into a community relationship.

AwarenessParticipationCommunityOngoing support

02 / Audience

A message for people, professionals and local businesses.

Community-minded people could find belonging and volunteering opportunities; professionals could access networking and development; existing supporters could deepen their connection with Sheffield Mind. The work therefore combines an empathetic cause-led message with practical reasons to get involved.

03 / Paid social

Invite businesses into a better mental-health conversation.

The paid-social concept directs local professionals and businesses to a free webinar, using clear action language and practical workplace benefits. The visual system adapts the same message for Facebook, Instagram feed and Story placements.

04 / LinkedIn

Tell a richer story for a professional audience.

The LinkedIn carousel expands the free-webinar message into an educational sequence: why workplace mental health matters, expert perspectives and a clear registration call to action. It is designed to move from awareness into consideration.

05 / Conversion

Make corporate support tangible.

This conversion-page concept frames a Corporate Support Scheme around the benefits of participation: showcasing a brand for good, networking, employee engagement and positive community impact. It gives local businesses a clear route to become a partner.

06 / Email journey

Keep the relationship moving after the first action.

The brief defines three lifecycle touchpoints: a welcome email that introduces ways to make a difference, a get-involved email that highlights volunteering, training and events, and a community update that promotes upcoming activity.

No email artwork was supplied with the project files, so this section documents the planned customer journey rather than adding unverified visuals.

07 / Outcome

Awareness and fundraising working together.

The campaign connected social, search and local awareness activity with a conversion route into events, volunteering and ongoing supporter engagement.

£50,000raised through documented marketing and outreach efforts.

No additional historical performance figures were supplied, so reach, engagement, conversion and registration metrics have not been presented as actual results.
