Digital Detox

A gamified mobile app concept built in four days — turning screen-time reduction into something measurable, motivating and rewarding.

Project overview

Digital Detox is a concept mobile application designed to help 18–35 year-olds reduce screen time and create a healthier balance between digital and real-world activity. The product uses gamification to make behaviour change feel achievable and rewarding rather than restrictive.

01 / The problem

Screen time is easy to measure. Changing behaviour is harder.

Simply showing someone how much time they spend on their phone does not give them a compelling reason to change. The concept explored a different question:

What if reducing screen time felt like completing a challenge rather than giving something up?

The answer was a gamified experience where users set goals, track progress, earn rewards and turn saved time into something tangible.

02 / The design sprint

From idea to MVP in four days.

The project was completed as a focused four-day design sprint in 2024. The tight timeframe required prioritising the core experience over feature expansion — and demonstrated how accumulated design knowledge enables faster, more confident decision-making.

03 / Onboarding

Get started with one tap.

The welcome screen introduces the product identity and gets users into the experience with the minimum possible friction. The Detox logo — a balanced, stacked form — gives the app a calm, approachable personality from the first screen.

04 / Dashboard

Everything the user needs, visible at once.

The homepage was designed to communicate the app's mission while providing direct access to all core tools. The Companion card surfaces a motivational message; the Community Challenge and Detox Score give users a quick performance read; Task Quickie supports in-the-moment focus sessions.

05 / Companion

Support that feels present, not intrusive.

The Companion provides dynamic motivational quotes, tips and timely reminders. It gives the product a more human personality — ongoing encouragement that keeps users focused without relying on system notifications alone. The dynamic logo humanises the experience.

06 / Goal setting

Set a goal. Keep it visible. Stay on track.

Goal setting was designed to be quick and easy, using a familiar progress-indicator pattern similar to a media player. Users set their daily screen-time limit, configure Pomodoro sessions and choose their meditation time. The persistent progress bar at the bottom of the screen keeps the active goal visible without requiring extra navigation.

07 / Gamification + Rewards

Turn saved time into something real.

The gamification system rewards users for every hour they spend away from their phone. The dot-grid progress visualisation makes accumulation satisfying and legible at a glance. For every 100 hours saved, users unlock exclusive rewards — turning abstract behaviour change into a tangible goal worth working towards.

08 / Progress + Account

A score that reflects real effort.

The Account screen consolidates everything into a single progress overview: Detox Score, Focus Ninja badge, daily streaks, badges collected and total time saved. This gives users a sense of identity within the app and reinforces their progress as something worth maintaining.

09 / Full device view

A coherent system across every screen.

The product visual language — orange accents, clean type, warm neutrals and the distinctive dot-grid — creates a consistent experience that feels energetic without being overwhelming. The tilted device presentation shows both primary views in context.

10 / Key decisions

Four decisions that shaped the product.

Keep the goal visible

Progress stays prominent instead of hiding behind a separate screen. Users need continuous awareness to maintain behaviour change.

Use familiar patterns

Progress indicators and reward systems borrow from interfaces users already understand, reducing the learning curve.

Add a human Companion

Ongoing encouragement through the Companion makes the experience feel supportive rather than like a restriction tool.

Reward time saved

Users gain something real from the behaviour change — they are not simply losing access to their phone.

No user-research sample size, usability-test results or validated behavioural outcomes were provided for this project. The case study is presented around the product strategy, design decisions and MVP deliverables.
