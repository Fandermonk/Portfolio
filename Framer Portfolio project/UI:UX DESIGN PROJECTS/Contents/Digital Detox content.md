# Digital Detox

## A gamified wellbeing app for building a healthier relationship with screens

**Project type:** UX/UI design sprint · Mobile app concept  
**Timeline:** Four-day design sprint, 2024  
**Audience:** Adults aged 18–35 who want practical support to reduce screen time

Digital Detox is a concept mobile app designed to help people spend less time scrolling and more time doing the things that matter to them. Rather than treating reduced screen time as a restriction, the experience makes progress visible, manageable and rewarding through small daily actions, supportive prompts and gamified milestones.

## The challenge

Many people want to reduce their screen time, but the tools available to them can feel punitive or easy to ignore. The design challenge was to create an encouraging experience that helps users set a realistic goal, see their progress at a glance and return to the app without adding more friction to their day.

## My focus

I created the product concept, interaction flow and mobile UI during a focused design sprint. My priorities were:

- A clear first-screen experience that immediately explains the app’s purpose
- Lightweight goal setting and progress tracking
- Positive motivation rather than guilt-based notifications
- A visual system that feels calm, optimistic and energetic
- Features that turn short offline moments into repeatable habits

## The solution

Digital Detox centres the experience around a dashboard that combines a daily screen-time summary, a current challenge and a one-tap focus task. A persistent progress card at the bottom of the interface works like a familiar media player: it makes an active goal easy to resume without forcing the user through extra navigation.

The concept brings together four supporting features:

### A daily companion

Short prompts, motivational quotes and practical tips provide a gentle nudge when users need it. The companion is designed to make the product feel present without becoming intrusive.

### Goals and visible progress

Users can set a screen-time goal quickly and track it through simple visual feedback. The dashboard prioritises the information needed to decide what to do next: time saved, time remaining and the current task.

### Rewards for real-world time

Gamification gives users a reason to keep going. Saving screen time contributes to unlocks, streaks and potential reward milestones, reframing digital wellbeing as a positive achievement.

### Focus and community challenges

Pomodoro-style focus sessions, quick tasks, simple statistics and community challenges help users break a larger goal into smaller wins. This keeps the product useful for both intentional work and everyday screen-time habits.

## Design decisions

**Make the next action obvious.** The home screen uses a clear hierarchy: current status first, then a challenge or task the user can begin immediately.

**Keep progress within reach.** The ongoing-goal card remains visually prominent so a user can pick up where they left off.

**Balance energy with calm.** A restrained neutral interface with vivid orange progress accents signals momentum without creating another visually exhausting feed.

**Reward consistency, not perfection.** The concept supports gradual improvement, which is more realistic for people trying to change a daily habit.

## Reflection

Working to a four-day deadline reinforced the value of a focused product story. I learned how to turn a broad wellbeing problem into a small set of clear, testable interactions, and how thoughtful visual hierarchy can make a habit-building experience feel lighter and more approachable.
