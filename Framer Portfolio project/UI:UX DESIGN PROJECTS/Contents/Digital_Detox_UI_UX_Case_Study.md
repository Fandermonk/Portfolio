# Digital Detox — Life Beyond the Screen

## A gamified approach to reducing screen time and improving life balance

**Project Type:** UI/UX / Product Design  
**Format:** Four-day design sprint  
**Year:** 2024  
**Product:** Digital Detox — Concept Mobile App  
**Target Audience:** 18–35 year-olds  
**Focus:** Gamification, habit building, screen-time reduction, motivation and real-world activities

---

## 01 — Project Overview

**Digital Detox** is a concept mobile application designed to help people reduce screen time and create a healthier balance between digital and real-world activities.

The concept uses **gamification** to make reducing screen time feel simple, measurable and rewarding.

Instead of framing digital detox as simply "using your phone less", the product encourages users to turn saved screen time into opportunities to do more in the real world.

> **Life beyond the screen.**

The core product loop is:

**Reduce screen time → Save time → Track progress → Earn rewards → Stay motivated**

The project was completed as a focused **four-day design sprint in 2024**, with the goal of rapidly turning an idea into a usable MVP concept.

# 03 — The Problem

Screen time is easy to measure, but changing behaviour is harder.

Simply telling someone that they spend too much time on their phone does not necessarily give them a compelling reason to change.

The concept therefore explored:

> **What if reducing screen time felt like completing a challenge rather than giving something up?**

This led to a gamified experience where users can:

- Set screen-time goals
- Track their progress
- Receive encouragement
- Discover motivational content
- Earn rewards for reducing screen time
- Turn saved time into something tangible

---

# 04 — Target Users

The concept was designed for people aged **18–35**.

The audience was considered digitally connected and comfortable with mobile experiences, gaming mechanics and reward systems.

### User Characteristics

- Tech-savvy
- Comfortable with mobile apps
- Familiar with gaming mechanics
- Familiar with digital rewards
- Interested in self-improvement
- Potentially motivated by measurable progress

The design therefore uses familiar digital patterns while redirecting them towards healthier habits.

# 07 — Feature: Companion

## A companion for constant support and daily inspiration

### Feature

The **Companion** provides dynamic support through:

- Motivational quotes
- Tips
- Encouragement
- Timely reminders

### Reasoning

Breaking an established screen habit requires ongoing guidance rather than a single intervention.

The Companion provides consistent support to keep users focused and motivated.

### Dynamic Logo

The Companion uses a dynamic logo concept to **humanise the experience**.

Rather than relying entirely on conventional productivity UI, the Companion gives the product a more approachable personality.

> **Design note:** The source project material states that the logo inspiration was borrowed from Headspace. In a portfolio, this should be described transparently as an inspiration/reference rather than presented as original branding.

---

# 08 — Companion Interaction

The Companion was designed to feel dynamic rather than like a static notification system.

### Active State

New content can appear as the user interacts with the experience.

This creates a sense that the Companion is an active part of the product rather than simply another information panel.

### Design Principle

**Support should feel present, not intrusive.**

---

# 09 — Feature: Goal Setting

## A prominent CTA which also works like one

Goal setting was designed to be quick and easy, with a visible tracking card.

The interaction uses a familiar progress pattern, similar to the way a music player displays ongoing progress.

### Feature

Users can:

- Set a screen-time goal
- View progress
- Continue an existing goal
- Understand their current status quickly

### Reasoning

Setting a goal can be easy, but tracking it consistently can be difficult.

By placing ongoing goals prominently at the bottom of the interface, users can quickly pick up where they left off.

This reduces friction and keeps the goal visible.

---

# 10 — Progress Tracking

The progress system was designed around persistent visibility.

Instead of requiring users to navigate into a separate tracking area, the current goal remains accessible within the main experience.

### Design Principle

> **Make progress visible.**

This supports:

- Quick recognition
- Reduced navigation
- Continuous awareness
- Lower interaction friction

---

# 11 — Feature: Gamification

## Turn saved screen time into rewards

Gamification is one of the core product ideas.

The concept rewards users for reducing their screen time and saving hours.

### Example Reward Mechanic

A user could receive a promotional reward for every **100 hours saved**.

The reward system gives users something tangible to work towards instead of relying only on abstract progress indicators.

### Reasoning

The product targets tech-savvy users who may already understand:

- Games
- Challenges
- Progress systems
- Rewards
- Unlockable achievements

The experience therefore borrows familiar mechanics from gaming and applies them to behaviour change.

# 18 — Key Design Decisions

### Decision 01 — Keep the Goal Visible

Instead of hiding progress behind another screen, the goal remains prominent.

**Why:** Users need continuous awareness to maintain behaviour change.

### Decision 02 — Use Familiar Progress Patterns

The progress interaction takes inspiration from interfaces users already understand, including media-player-style progress indicators.

**Why:** Familiar patterns can reduce the learning curve.

### Decision 03 — Add a Human Companion

The product introduces a Companion rather than relying entirely on system notifications.

**Why:** Ongoing encouragement can make the experience feel more supportive.

### Decision 04 — Reward Time Saved

The concept connects reduced screen time with tangible rewards.

**Why:** The user gains something from the behaviour change rather than simply losing access to their phone.

# 19 — Design Sprint Constraints

The project was completed within a **four-day design sprint**.

The compressed timeframe created a significant constraint: there was not enough time to explore every possible direction.

The process therefore focused on:

- The core problem
- The MVP
- Essential features
- Interaction patterns
- Visual hierarchy
- Product storytelling

This helped prevent unnecessary feature expansion.

# 20 — Reflection

Working within tight time constraints was a significant challenge, but it also demonstrated the compounding effect of years spent learning design, understanding processes and building a repository of ideas and visuals.

The project showed how accumulated experience can enable faster decision-making and a more focused design process.

It reinforced a key lesson:

> **Speed does not necessarily come from skipping the design process. It comes from knowing the process well enough to make decisions confidently.**

---

# 21 — What I Learned

### Constraints Can Improve Focus
A short sprint forces prioritisation.

### MVP Does Not Mean Minimal Thinking
The product may be small, but each feature still needs a reason to exist.

### Behaviour Change Needs Motivation
Tracking alone may not be enough. The product needs reasons for users to continue.

### Familiar Patterns Reduce Friction
Using interactions users already understand can make a new product easier to learn.

### Gamification Should Support the Goal
Gamification should reinforce the intended behaviour rather than becoming the product itself.
