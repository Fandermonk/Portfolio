# Furnich AR

## Making flat-pack furniture assembly clearer with augmented reality

**Project type:** UX/UI design · Augmented-reality app concept  
**Audience:** First-time furniture buyers, students, young adults and DIY-minded shoppers

Furnich AR is a mobile concept that makes furniture assembly more approachable. Instead of asking people to decipher paper manuals, search for the right video or hire a handyman, the app uses guided AR steps to show what to do, where a part belongs and which tool is needed.

## The problem

Flat-pack furniture promises affordability and flexibility, but assembly is often confusing, time-consuming and frustrating. Traditional instructions are difficult to follow; online tutorials can be hard to find and repeatedly pause; and professional help introduces extra cost and scheduling uncertainty.

The opportunity was to give people a more confident, self-guided way to build furniture while supporting local flat-pack retailers.

## Research and insight

With two fellow designers, I helped conduct interviews with four participants: young adults setting up a first home and DIY enthusiasts seeking a more affordable assembly process. Three recurring needs shaped the product:

- Instructions needed to be clearer and more visual than a printed manual.
- Users wanted support in the moment, without hunting for a video or a professional.
- People needed to know the tools and hardware required before starting.

## The solution

Furnich AR connects a physical product to a guided mobile experience. A customer scans a store QR code or enters a serial number, selects their furniture and follows a visual assembly path.

### Find furniture quickly

The home experience uses store and product cards with category filters for chairs, sofas, beds and desks. This gives users enough context to make a choice quickly while keeping the interface focused and easy to scan.

### Understand the product before assembly

The product-detail view combines a large furniture image, key product information and a clear checklist of required tools. It reduces uncertainty before a user commits to the build.

### Build with contextual AR guidance

The AR mode is designed to overlay step-by-step instructions onto the physical furniture. It identifies placement, direction and the hardware needed at each stage, replacing vague diagrams with instruction in context.

### Get support without leaving the flow

An AI assistant offers quick help with tools, steps and troubleshooting. Voice and gesture controls were considered for hands-free navigation when a user is actively assembling furniture.

## Design decisions

**A card-led home screen** makes stores and products visual, concise and faster to browse than a text-heavy directory.

**A prominently placed QR action** reduces the work needed to move from a physical box to its correct guide.

**Tools are surfaced before the first step** so users can prepare instead of being interrupted halfway through the process.

**Minimal UI lets the AR experience lead.** Soft backgrounds, familiar icons and a compact bottom navigation keep the interface calm while the furniture and instructions remain the focus.

## Reflection

This project was my first deeper exploration of designing for augmented reality. It taught me to balance technical possibility with clear interaction design, and to document a real-time experience in a way that would support a smooth developer handoff.
