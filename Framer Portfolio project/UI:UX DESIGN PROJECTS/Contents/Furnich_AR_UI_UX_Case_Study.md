# Furnich AR — UI/UX Case Study

## Life Beyond the Manual

**Furnich AR** is an augmented reality mobile app concept designed to make flat-pack furniture assembly easier, more interactive, and less frustrating. Instead of relying on confusing manuals, lengthy video tutorials, or costly handymen, the product uses AR to provide step-by-step, real-time assembly guidance.

**Role:** Product Design Lead & Product Designer  
**Timeline:** January 2021 – August 2021  
**Duration:** 8 months  
**Project Type:** Mobile App / UI/UX / Augmented Reality

---

## Overview

Ready-to-assemble furniture, also known as flat-pack, knock-down, kit, or self-assembly furniture, gives customers the flexibility to build products themselves. However, the assembly experience can become difficult when instructions are unclear or users cannot identify the correct parts, tools, or sequence.

Furnich AR was designed around a simple idea:

> Make furniture assembly feel like following a visual guide in the real world rather than interpreting a manual.

The app combines furniture discovery with interactive AR assembly guidance, QR-based product access, visual hardware identification, and hands-free interaction.

---

## The Problem

### Furniture assembly is confusing, costly, and time-consuming

Users assembling furniture commonly have three options:

- Follow the physical instruction manual.
- Search online for a suitable video tutorial.
- Pay a handyman to assemble the furniture.

Each approach has limitations. Manuals can be difficult to interpret, online videos can require repeated searching, pausing, and replaying, and hiring help introduces additional cost and depends on availability.

The project focused on technology-aware users who wanted to complete the task themselves but needed clearer guidance.

### User frustrations

> “Finding a handyman is expensive and inconvenient. I wish there was an easier way to assemble furniture!”

— Liam, Masters Student

> “Furniture manuals are a nightmare! The instructions are unclear, and I always end up frustrated.”

— Galen, Student and Startup Founder

---

## The Goal

The goal was to simplify furniture assembly through an interactive, step-by-step augmented reality experience.

Furnich AR aimed to help users:

1. Access the correct furniture guide quickly.
2. Understand which parts and tools are required.
3. Follow assembly instructions directly in their physical environment.
4. Complete assembly without depending on a handyman.
5. Get additional support when they become stuck.

---

# Research

## Market Context

The project began by examining how people currently assemble ready-to-assemble furniture.

Existing solutions included physical manuals, online resources such as YouTube and furniture websites, and services that connect customers with handymen.

The research also considered the appeal of DIY furniture among younger users. The project referenced the **IKEA Effect**, the idea that people can place greater value on something when they have invested effort into creating it.

This supported the broader opportunity: rather than removing the DIY process, Furnich AR could make the process easier to understand and more enjoyable.

---

## User Research

To understand how people approached furniture assembly and where existing solutions failed, I conducted user interviews with the help of two designers.

**Participants:** 4  
**Participant profile:** Young adults setting up their first apartments and DIY enthusiasts looking for cost-effective assembly solutions.

### Key insights

#### 01 — Traditional guides create confusion

Users struggled with unclear manuals and vague instructions, which could lead to frustration during assembly.

#### 02 — Video tutorials interrupt the process

Finding the correct video and repeatedly pausing or replaying individual steps made the process slower.

#### 03 — Hiring help can be costly and inconvenient

Users who preferred DIY solutions wanted to avoid the additional cost and availability constraints associated with handymen.

#### 04 — Users wanted real-time guidance

An interactive, visual guide was identified as a desirable way to make assembly easier without adding another service cost.

### Primary need

> “I want an easy, cost-effective way to assemble furniture without hiring expensive handymen or struggling with complex manuals.”

### Experience expectation

> “I seek a step-by-step, interactive AR guide to make furniture assembly quick, frustration-free, and enjoyable.”

# Product Requirements

Based on the research and user tasks, four core requirements were identified.

### QR Code Scanning

Users can scan a QR code provided by a store to access the relevant interactive assembly instructions.

### Step-by-Step AR Guidance

AR overlays provide real-time guidance directly on the furniture, showing users where components should be positioned.

### Tool & Hardware Identification

Visual indicators communicate which screws, bolts, and tools are required at each step.

### Hands-Free Voice & Gesture Controls

Voice and gesture controls allow users to navigate instructions without repeatedly touching their phone while assembling furniture.

---

# Product Experience

The experience was designed around a straightforward journey:

**Discover furniture → Select product → View details → Prepare tools → Scan/access guide → Use AR guidance → Assemble**

The interface needed to support both furniture discovery and the practical task of assembly without overwhelming the user with unnecessary information.

---

# Key Design Decision 01

## Keep the home experience simple and focused

Early concepts explored more complex layouts influenced by e-commerce and directory-style experiences.

These iterations included:

- Detailed filtering
- Search functionality
- Bookmarking
- More information-heavy layouts

However, through user testing, the direction shifted toward a simpler browsing experience.

The final approach prioritized:

- Key information upfront
- Clear navigation
- Scannable furniture cards
- Category-based filtering
- Easy access to saved items and other core functions

### Furniture cards

Each store/furniture card provides a quick preview before the user opens the full experience.

The cards communicate:

- Store image
- Store name
- Specialty

This gives users enough context to decide whether to explore further without forcing them through additional screens.

### Category filters

Categories such as:

- Chairs
- Sofas
- Beds
- Desks

help users narrow the browsing experience.

The card-based approach was selected instead of a text-heavy list because it created a more visual and scannable way to explore furniture.

### Navigation

The bottom navigation provides quick access to:

- Home
- Notifications
- Bookmarks
- Settings

---

# Iteration

## From feature-heavy to focused

The early home-screen concepts attempted to provide users with many discovery and filtering capabilities.

The later direction reduced visual complexity and prioritized the actions users needed most.

This iteration reinforced an important UX principle for the project:

**More functionality does not automatically create a better experience.**

The interface needed to help users reach their goal quickly rather than make them navigate through unnecessary options.

---

# Finding Furniture — Product Details

The product detail experience was designed to bridge furniture discovery and assembly preparation.

It provides users with:

- A detailed furniture preview
- Product information
- Essential tools
- AI-powered assistance
- AR visualization

The product page therefore acts as a preparation point before the user begins assembly.

---

# Key Design Decision 02

## Make the required tools visible before assembly

The product display was kept visually clean while giving users the information they needed to prepare.

The essential tools section presents the required tools using clear visual icons.

This helps users understand what they need before starting rather than discovering halfway through the assembly process that they are missing something.

### Design principle

**Prepare first. Assemble second.**

---

# Key Design Decision 03

## Add an AI Assistant for contextual support

An AI chatbot assistant was incorporated into the product experience as an additional support layer.

The assistant was designed to provide:

- Tool usage guidance
- Step-by-step assembly help
- Troubleshooting support

The purpose was not to replace the AR experience, but to provide an additional source of help when users encountered uncertainty.

This created a layered support model:

**AR guidance → Visual information → AI assistance**

---

# AR Visualization

After selecting a furniture product, users can use AR to visualize how the furniture fits within their space.

This extends the product experience beyond assembly and gives users a way to interact with the furniture in their environment.

The AR concept supports two important moments:

### Before assembly

Visualize the furniture in the user's environment.

### During assembly

Use AR overlays to understand where components belong and how they should be positioned.

---

# Key Design Decision 04

## Prioritize fast access to the QR flow

The QR scanner was treated as an important entry point into the assembly experience.

The QR icon was positioned in the top-right area of the main interface so users could access scanning quickly.

The flow also considered manual serial-number entry as an alternative.

### Intended experience

**Scan QR → Identify furniture → Access guide → Begin assembly**

This reduced the need for users to search manually for the correct furniture instructions.

---

# Interaction Model

The project explored interaction beyond conventional touch-based mobile navigation.

### Touch

Used for browsing, selecting products, accessing details, and navigating the application.

### AR

Used to provide spatial instructions and visualize furniture in the user's environment.

### Voice

Designed to support hands-free navigation during assembly.

### Gesture

Considered as another hands-free interaction method while the user's hands are occupied.

Together, these interactions were intended to make the assembly experience more natural and task-focused.

---

# UX Principles

Several principles guided the design direction.

### Reduce cognitive load

Instructions should communicate what the user needs to do now rather than forcing them to interpret a large amount of information.

### Make information visual

Furniture parts, tools, and instructions should be communicated visually wherever possible.

### Keep important actions accessible

QR scanning and essential navigation should not be hidden behind unnecessary layers.

### Support users when they get stuck

AR guidance and the AI assistant provide additional support when users need clarification.

### Design for the physical environment

Unlike a conventional content application, the interface is used while the user is physically assembling furniture. The design therefore needs to account for attention, movement, and hands-free interaction.

# What I Learned

Furnich AR was my first extensive project working with augmented reality, and it significantly changed how I approached interaction design.

### Designing for real-world interaction

AR interfaces behave differently from conventional mobile interfaces. The design needs to account for the user's physical environment, movement, attention, and context.

### Balancing innovation with accessibility

An AR product can easily become visually impressive but difficult to use. The challenge was to keep the interaction simple enough that the technology supported the task rather than becoming the task.

### Research before designing

The project reinforced the importance of understanding how people currently solve a problem before deciding what the product should do.

### Working within technical constraints

Collaboration with developers helped me understand how AR interactions and UI components need to be documented for implementation.

### Designing under complexity

The project required connecting multiple systems — furniture discovery, QR identification, AR guidance, tool information, voice/gesture interaction, and AI assistance — into one coherent experience.
